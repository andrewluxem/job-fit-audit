# Interview prep

The answer side of the profile. Use it for Step 4d and interview mode. Every story and number comes from `profile.md` or `story-bank.md`; this file supplies structure, not facts.

## Tell me about yourself (90 seconds)

Three beats, and land on why this room. Never recite the resume in order.

1. **What you do.** One sentence of positioning from the profile, plus years and two or three recognizable employers.
2. **Proof, two numbers max,** chosen for this JD. Recent first.
3. **Why this room.** One specific thing about their business that you actually looked at, not their mission statement.

Keep a variant per positioning angle in the profile (e.g. consumer vs B2B, builder vs scaler).

## Why you're available, and why us

**Why available.** Two lines, no drama, then pivot to a result. Never criticize a past employer. Layoffs and restructures are normal; say it plainly.

**Why them.** Three parts in order: something specific about their business you looked at (their emails, app, ads, onboarding, pricing page), the part of the role that matches what you've built before, and the honest personal fit (scope, level, location). Name the work, not the values page.

## The behavioral set

Prepare one story for each, drawn from the story bank. STAR, with the Result carrying a verified number.

- **Strength.** A real, specific capability with two proof stories, not an adjective.
- **Weakness.** A real tradeoff you manage, with what you do about it. Not a humblebrag.
- **Failure.** Something that actually didn't work, what you learned, what you do differently now.
- **Pressure.** A deadline or launch where the stakes were visible.
- **Conflict or disagreement.** A time you pushed back with data, and whether you won or disagreed-and-committed.
- **Ownership beyond the role.** A gap you filled that wasn't yours.
- **Managing people** (if the role has reports). How you develop people, hire, and handle underperformance.
- **Scaling yourself.** Enablement, documentation, training, systems.

## Values mapping

If the company publishes values or leadership principles, map one story to each and prepare a question back about how they live it. Don't restate the value in the answer; let the story show it.

## Frameworks

When the conversation turns strategic ("how would you approach our first 90 days?"), a named, real framework the user has used beats improvisation. Pull these from the story bank or ask the user for them. A few generic structures that work for most marketing roles:

- **30/60/90:** listen and audit; quick wins plus a plan; first measurable result.
- **Audit order:** data and measurement first, then the customer journey, then the channels, then the team and stack.
- **Growth levers for a customer base:** grow the active base, increase frequency, increase order size or expansion, monetize adjacencies, all managed against defined KPIs with a test-and-learn loop.
- **The campaign brief questions:** what's happening in the market, what is this for, who is targeted and why, key message, desired response, creative and timing, budget.

## Questions for them

Pick six to eight, weighted to the round. Never ask what the careers page answers.

**Role and success:** Is this new or a backfill, and if a backfill, why did the person leave? What outcomes do you want at 90 days, six months, a year? How is success measured? What separates good from great in this seat? What budget, headcount, and tools come with it? What decisions would be mine?

**Team and structure:** What does the team look like today? Which cross-functional partners matter most? Who does this role report to, and how does feedback work?

**Program and stack:** What's working now, and what's been tried and dropped? What platforms are you on, and is a change on the table? Where does customer data live? What tests have worked recently?

**Business and direction:** What are the biggest challenges in the next year? Where are the untapped opportunities? If you were me, where would you put attention first?

**Process (end of a screen):** What are the next stages and timeline? When do you want someone in the seat? Is there an internal candidate?

Company-specific questions beat all of these. Build two or three from real time on their product and marketing.

## Landmines

Short tenures, gaps, a step down in title, a layoff, a metric with an attribution caveat. For each, a two-sentence answer: the fact, then the pivot to value. Rehearse them out loud; they're only hard when they're a surprise.

## Compensation and offer

- Research their structure first: rigid bands or flexible, financial health, market reputation.
- Deflect early number questions when possible ("I'd like to understand the scope first; what's the range for the role?"). If pressed, give a researched range, not a single number.
- Lead with value delivered before any number.
- Negotiate total compensation: bonus target, equity, sign-on to bridge a base gap, severance terms, PTO, remote flexibility, development budget.
- Take the offer away and review it before countering.
- Weigh trajectory: ask what the path looked like for the last two people in the role.

## Round weighting

- **Recruiter screen:** opening, why available, why them, comp approach, logistics questions.
- **Hiring manager:** requirement-to-story map, frameworks, two company-specific ideas, role-and-success questions.
- **Panel:** behavioral set, one story per interviewer's likely focus, cross-functional questions.
- **Exec final:** business-level POV, 90-day plan, direction questions, landmines.
- **Comp conversation:** the compensation section only.
