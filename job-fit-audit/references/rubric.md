# Scoring rubric

Seven dimensions, 1 to 5 each, 35 max. Score against the user's profile, not against an ideal candidate. Inflated scores waste the user's hours; deflated scores cost them good roles. When torn between two scores, pick the lower one and say why in the verdict.

## Scope (what the role actually owns)

5: owns the outcome the user is best at, end to end, with a number attached (revenue, retention, pipeline, share, adoption).
4: owns most of it, or owns it inside a larger team with clear authority.
3: the right work, but shared ownership or unclear authority.
2: the user's discipline is a support function here (lifecycle that exists to serve demand gen; brand that only produces sales decks).
1: the job is mostly something else under a familiar title.

What "owns it end to end" means by discipline:

| Discipline | A 5 looks like |
|---|---|
| Lifecycle / CRM / Retention | Owns retention or repeat revenue, the customer journeys, and the messaging platform |
| Growth / Performance | Owns acquisition targets and budget across paid channels, with CAC/ROAS accountability |
| Brand | Owns positioning, identity, and campaigns with budget and agency relationships |
| Product Marketing | Owns positioning, launches, and sales enablement for a product line, with a seat in roadmap decisions |
| Content / SEO | Owns organic strategy and a content engine tied to traffic or pipeline goals |
| Demand Gen / ABM | Owns pipeline targets and the programs that create them |
| Marketing Ops / Martech | Owns the stack, data flows, and attribution, with authority to change them |
| Social / Community | Owns channels and community programs with engagement or revenue goals |
| Comms / PR | Owns media strategy, spokesperson prep, and crisis response |
| CMO / generalist | Owns the marketing P&L and team |

## Level

5: matches the core target level and scope.
4: stretch level the user can credibly reach, or core level with slightly narrower scope.
3: one step below core with compensating strengths (remote, strong comp, great company).
2: at the user's floor with none of their stated conditions met.
1: well below floor, or title inflation (leadership title, IC scope, junior comp).

## Toolkit

5: the user's "scores 5" tools or methods are named, or the JD describes a problem they've solved before (a platform migration, a rebrand, a 0-to-1 launch). A migration they have done before is a 5 even if the destination platform is new to them.
4: adjacent tools with clear transfer (one ESP to another, one BI tool to another).
3: toolkit not specified, or mixed.
2: mostly unfamiliar tools, some transfer.
1: a specialized toolkit they've never touched, and the JD treats it as day-one required.

Common toolkits by discipline, to help map JD language to the profile: lifecycle (Braze, Klaviyo, Iterable, Salesforce Marketing Cloud, HubSpot, Attentive, Customer.io); growth (Google Ads, Meta, TikTok, AppsFlyer, MMM/incrementality); product marketing (Gong, Highspot, Klue, pricing research); content/SEO (Ahrefs, Semrush, GSC, CMSs); ops (Salesforce, Marketo, HubSpot, Segment, dbt, SQL); social (Sprout, Later, creator platforms). These are examples, not a checklist.

## Comp

5: posted range at or above the user's target.
4: range overlaps target.
3: unposted, or range straddles their floor. Unposted is neutral, not negative.
2: top of range near their floor.
1: top of range below their floor.

If the profile says "case by case," still score it and report what you saw; never auto-skip on comp unless comp is a listed dealbreaker.

## Location

5: exactly matches the user's rules (remote when they want remote; local hybrid when that's allowed).
3: remote with travel above what's typical for the level, or hybrid with an unclear cadence.
1 to 2: requires regular presence they said they won't do.

"Remote" that's restricted to certain states or time zones: check against the profile before scoring.

## Referral path

5: a warm contact inside the company, or the user worked there.
4: alumni of a past employer in the company or near the hiring manager; an active community tie.
3: second-degree connection likely findable.
2: nothing visible yet, but the company is in the user's industry circle.
1: pure cold.

Name the path concretely in the verdict ("ex-[Employer] alumni in their growth org"), not generically.

## Freshness

5: posted under 48 hours ago.
4: under a week.
3: one to three weeks, or no date visible.
2: three-plus weeks.
1: reposted repeatedly, or evergreen-looking (always hiring, vague scope, no comp). Ghost-posting risk.

## Tiers

A = 30+: APPLY NOW. B = 24 to 29: WORTH APPLYING. C = under 24: SKIP. Use the user's custom cutoffs if their profile sets them.

A single 1 in Scope or Level usually means the role is wrong even if the total clears B; say so in the verdict.
